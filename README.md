# Correlated Disorder and the Radiative Loss of a Photonic-Crystal Quasi-Bound State in the Continuum

**Authors:** Hitesh Kumar Singh · [ORCID: 0009-0008-1583-4848](https://orcid.org/0009-0008-1583-4848), and Rajesh Sharma
*Department of Physics, Kurukshetra University, Kurukshetra, Haryana, India (H. K. Singh); Department of Physics, MNS Government College, Bhiwani, Haryana, India (R. Sharma)*

---

## Abstract

Fabrication disorder turns a symmetry-protected bound state in the continuum (BIC) of a photonic-crystal slab into a quasi-BIC. Guided-mode-expansion calculations of 680 disorder realisations in supercells with one-dimensional Gaussian-correlated hole-radius disorder show that, at fixed disorder amplitude, correlation lengths of one to two lattice constants raise the mean radiative loss about 1.7-fold relative to uncorrelated disorder; the loss scales as the amplitude squared. A spectral-overlap model predicts the enhancement and its growth with supercell size without adjustable parameters. Enhancement ratios change by up to 5% with the plane-wave cutoff and absolute losses by up to 13%, and an apparent merged BIC proves to be a basis-truncation artefact.

## Core Objectives

- Determine how the correlation length of fabrication disorder changes the radiative loss of a symmetry-protected BIC at fixed disorder amplitude.
- Provide a physical model for the dependence.
- Establish which computed quantities are converged in the guided-mode-expansion basis, and test whether an apparent merged BIC survives enlargement of the basis.

## Scientific Significance

Measured quality factors of quasi-BICs fall short of ideal values, and fabrication disorder is typically spatially correlated, for example through proximity effects in lithography. This project shows that the root-mean-square error alone does not set the loss: correlation over one to two lattice constants is more harmful than uncorrelated disorder of the same amplitude. It also shows the convergence checks needed before a high-Q or merged-BIC claim can be trusted.

## Methodological Approach

- **Guided-mode expansion** with the open-source legume implementation, on N × 1 supercells (N = 10, 12, 16) with Gaussian-correlated hole-radius disorder generated by circulant spectral synthesis.
- **680 realisations** in 24 ensembles over three amplitudes and four correlation lengths. Two loss estimators were compared.
- **Statistics:** percentile bootstrap intervals, Bonferroni correction, random-effects pooling with Cochran's heterogeneity test, and a two-way fit.
- **Verification:** zero-contrast limit, the α⁻² law, and an independent RCWA comparison on a stripe grating (agreement within 4.3% in Q).
- **Model:** a spectral-overlap model whose enhancement prediction contains no free parameter, and a cutoff study from 4.001 to 8.001 in units of 2π/a.

## Contributions

1. A quantitative measurement of the effect of correlation length on quasi-BIC radiative loss, with a pooled enhancement of about 1.7 for correlation lengths of one and two lattice constants.
2. A spectral-overlap model that explains the enhancement as a transfer of spectral weight toward folded modes close to the BIC in frequency, and reproduces its growth with supercell size.
3. A separation of quantities converged in the plane-wave cutoff (enhancement ratios) from those that are not (absolute losses).
4. A demonstration that an apparent merged BIC at low cutoff is a basis-truncation artefact.

5. **Positioning relative to prior work.** Correlated disorder has been studied in photonic-crystal waveguides, where long-range correlations reduced out-of-plane loss (Vasco and Hughes, *ACS Photonics*, 2019), and disorder-generated quasi-bound flat bands have been studied in photonic-crystal slabs (arXiv:2511.05024). A literature search in October 2026 found no earlier study of Gaussian-correlated hole-radius disorder acting on a symmetry-protected BIC with a spectral-overlap model.

## Key Findings

- Pooled enhancement over the three amplitudes: 1.72 [1.14, 2.61] at one lattice constant and 1.75 [1.42, 2.16] at two; no effect detected at half a lattice constant.
- Excess loss scales with σ with exponent 1.95 ± 0.10, consistent with second order.
- The enhancement grows with supercell size over the three sizes tested; the model's coupling constant, however, depends on N.
- Between cutoffs 4.001 and 8.001 the mean loss falls by a factor of 1.74, while enhancement ratios change by at most about 5%.
- The Q advantage of the apparent merged-BIC structure falls from 13,319 to 1.45 as the cutoff increases, and its asymptotic exponent is −2 at every cutoff.

## Scope and Limitations

The disorder is one-dimensional, only hole radii fluctuate, and the slab is free-standing and lossless. The perturbative radiative rates are validated against RCWA only up to Q ≈ 1.8 × 10⁴, whereas the ensemble means correspond to Q between 6.8 × 10⁴ and 3.1 × 10⁶. Three supercell sizes do not establish the large-N behaviour.
